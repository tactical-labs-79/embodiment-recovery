Embodiment Recovery Research

Independent research on robot dynamics modeling, generalization, and data-efficient adaptation through simulation-based experiments.

This project explores how machine learning models can learn robot dynamics, generalize to unseen conditions, and adapt to changes in physical dynamics.

The research has evolved through multiple experimental iterations, from initial dynamics modeling to more advanced investigations into robot adaptation.

Research Objectives
Develop and evaluate robot dynamics models.
Investigate prediction accuracy and generalization.
Evaluate model performance under changing physical dynamics.
Explore data-efficient adaptation strategies.
Compare generic adaptation with shift-specific adaptation.
Investigate how adaptation data affects model performance.
Research Progress

The project has progressed through 13 experimental versions, with each iteration exploring different aspects of robot dynamics modeling and adaptation.

V1–V7: Initial dynamics modeling, model evaluation, and generalization experiments.
V8–V10: Experiments investigating model adaptation under changes in physical dynamics.
V11–V12: Controlled experiments examining generic and shift-specific adaptation.
V13: Latest experimental iteration. Further details and results will be documented as the research progresses.

This version overview is preliminary and will be refined as each experiment is documented.

Experimental Methodology

Experiments are conducted in robotic simulation using controlled conditions, multiple random seeds, and quantitative performance evaluation.

The research uses iterative experimentation to investigate model behavior, identify limitations, and evaluate potential improvements.

Research Status

Ongoing independent research.

New experimental versions, findings, and validated results will be added as the project develops.
